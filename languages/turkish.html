Turkish

Türkçe

Turkish is not Indo-European but belongs to the Turkic family, which reaches into Central Asia. It works by agglutination: suffixes stack at the end of the word, so that a single Turkish word can translate a whole English sentence. In 1928 it abandoned the Arabic alphabet for the Latin one, in a reform that also set aside a large part of its Arabic and Persian vocabulary.

How to spot it

The dotless ı and the silent ğ, plus ş and ç. An agglutinative language: suffixes stack up at the end of words.

A few sentences

  • Her sabah çay içerim, kahve değil.

    Every morning I drink tea, not coffee.

  • Bir bardak su alabilir miyim?

    Could I have a glass of water?

  • Şemsiyemi evde bıraktım.

    I have left my umbrella behind at home.

  • Bugün hava kapalı.

    It is overcast today.

  • Telefonum çalmıyor.

    My phone is not ringing.
