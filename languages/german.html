German

Deutsch

German has more native speakers than any other language in the European Union. Its most visible quirk is also the easiest to spot: every common noun takes a capital letter, a rule no other major language follows. It keeps four grammatical cases, and its habit of welding nouns together produces the famously long compounds. Dialects remain strong and far apart: a northern German will not spontaneously understand Swiss German.

How to spot it

Every common noun takes a capital letter. The ß, the umlauts ä ö ü and the endlessly compounded words are its signature.

A few sentences

  • Ich gehe jeden Morgen mit meinem Hund im Park spazieren.

    I walk my dog in the park every morning.

  • Im Sommer fahren wir immer ans Meer.

    In summer we always go to the seaside.

  • Ich muss heute Abend noch die Wäsche waschen.

    I still have to do the laundry this evening.

  • Das Wetter soll morgen besser werden.

    The weather is supposed to get better tomorrow.

  • Ich würde gern ein Jahr im Ausland leben.

    I would like to live abroad for a year.
