Sicilian

Sicilianu

Sicilian is not a dialect of Italian but a Romance language in its own right, shaped by an island past in which Greek, Arab, Norman and Spanish rule followed one another. It keeps only three vowels at the end of words, a, i and u, where Italian uses five, which gives all those words in -u. It lives mostly in speech and remains strong in song and theatre.

How to spot it

Final -u where Italian has -o (beddu, amicu, travagghiu), double dd (beddu, picciriddi), “nun” for “non” and “ca” for “che”.

A few sentences

  • Stamatina m’arrisbigghiai prestu e fici cafè pi tutti.

    This morning I woke up early and made coffee for everyone.

  • Nun haiu vogghia di travagghiari oi, fa troppu cauddu.

    I don’t feel like working today, it is too hot.

  • Ora mi nni vaiu, è tardu e domani m’haiu a susiri prestu.

    I am leaving now, it is late and tomorrow I have to get up early.

  • Mi sapiti diri quantu costa stu pani?

    Could you tell me how much this bread costs?

  • Nun ci criu ca già passaru deci anni.

    I can’t believe ten years have already passed.
