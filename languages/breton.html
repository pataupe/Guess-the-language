Breton

Brezhoneg

Breton is not a descendant of Gaulish, contrary to a widespread belief: it was brought to Armorica by migrants from Britain in late antiquity. It is therefore a cousin of Welsh and Cornish, not of the speech of the Gauls. It is the only Celtic language still alive on the continent, and its speaker numbers fell sharply over the twentieth century.

How to spot it

A unique signature: the trigraph c’h (alc’hwez, c’hoari) and the zh of “Breizh”.

A few sentences

  • Demat deoc’h, penaos emañ kont ganeoc’h?

    Good day to you, how are things?

  • Ur banne kafe a vo mat.

    A cup of coffee would be welcome.

  • Yen eo an amzer.

    The weather is cold.

  • Mont a ran da welet ma c’hoar.

    I am going to see my sister.

  • Gwerzhet eo an ti.

    The house has been sold.
