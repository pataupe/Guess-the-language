Luxembourgish

Lëtzebuergesch

Luxembourgish is a Moselle Franconian variety that became a national language of Luxembourg in 1984, alongside French and German. It borrows heavily from French, often without altering the word, so a Germanic sentence can be studded with terms a French speaker recognises instantly. Its written standard is recent and usage is still less fixed than in long-standardised languages. Most of its speakers are trilingual.

How to spot it

German run through a French filter: plenty of ë and é, the pronoun “ech”, and French loanwords left untouched.

A few sentences

  • Ech hunn haut e laangen Dag gehat.

    I have had a long day today.

  • Ech hunn haut leider keng Zäit.

    I am afraid I have no time today.

  • Ech sinn haut ganz midd.

    I am very tired today.

  • Wou ass d’Post, wann ech gelift?

    Where is the post office, please?

  • D’Haus ass ze deier fir eis.

    The house is too expensive for us.
