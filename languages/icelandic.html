Icelandic

Íslenska

Icelandic has changed remarkably little since the Middle Ages: an Icelander today reads the thirteenth-century sagas without a translation. The island's isolation accounts for much of that, but so does deliberate policy: rather than borrow foreign words, Icelandic coins new ones from its own roots. It kept the letters þ and ð, which English once had and lost.

How to spot it

Impossible to mistake: the letters þ (thorn) and ð (eth), plus the endings -ur, -ið, -nn.

A few sentences

  • Ég ætla að fara í sund eftir vinnu í dag.

    I am going to the swimming pool after work today.

  • Ég tala ekki mjög góða ensku.

    I do not speak very good English.

  • Ég gleymdi að loka glugganum.

    I forgot to shut the window.

  • Ég á engan pening fyrr en á föstudaginn.

    I have no money until Friday.

  • Bíllinn minn bilaði í morgun.

    My car broke down this morning.
