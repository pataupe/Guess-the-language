Alsatian

Elsässisch

Alsatian mostly covers the Alemannic dialects of the Rhine plain, close to Swiss German and Baden German, with Franconian dialects in the north. It is passed on above all by speech and has no single written standard: each author adapts the spelling to their own valley. It still has several hundred thousand speakers, but its transmission to children has fallen sharply since 1945.

How to spot it

Germanic with no fixed spelling: ì, à, ü, ö (ìch, nìt, güet, Schwöschter), “mìr” for “wir”, “nìt” for “nicht” and verbs ending in -e (schlofe, màche).

A few sentences

  • Hit Morge ha ìch e güeter Kaffee gedrunke.

    This morning I had a good coffee.

  • S’rägnet scho widder, dìs Johr ìsch s’Wetter verruckt.

    It is raining again, the weather is crazy this year.

  • Hesch dü mini Schlissle gsehn?

    Have you seen my keys?

  • Dü muesch nìt àlles glaawe, wàs dü hersch.

    You shouldn’t believe everything you hear.

  • Kànnsch mer s’Salz gä?

    Can you pass me the salt?
